Counters….

Digital counters are sequential logic circuits used to count clock pulses. The modulus (or "Mod") refers to the total number of
unique states a counter goes through before resetting to its starting value.

Digital counters are fundamental building blocks in modern electronics. Their applications span from everyday consumer
electronics to advanced industrial automation, scientific instrumentation, and microprocessors. Ex: Digital Clocks and Watches,
Frequency Dividers & counters. Used in Microprocessors, Computers, and State Machines, used in Industrial Automation and

output of the last flip-flop is fed back into the input of the first flip-flop. Structure: It uses n D flip-flops for an n-state
counter, sharing a common clock signal. A single 1 (high) bit is preset into one flip-flop while the rest are set to 0 (low).
Rotation: With each clock pulse, the 1 bit shifts sequentially from one flip-flop to the next in a continuous loop. For an
n-bit ring counter, it cycles through n unique states.

The reasons for the counter to enter invalid states:
When power is first applied to the network, the initial states of flip-flops may be unpredictable

A counter in which all the states not included in the original counting sequence eventually lead to the
Normal counting sequence after one or more clock pulses are applied, is said to be Self Correcting.
To avoid having the counter “hang up”, (lock out), it should always be designed as self-correcting.

Describe the working principle of a JK master–slave flip-flop with suitable waveforms. Explain why the JK master–slave flip-
flop eliminates the race-around condition.
Ans:
The Master-Slave configuration eliminates race-around condition by splitting the flip-flop into two stages that respond to
opposite phases of the clock signal. Clock Pulse High: The Master is enabled. It accepts the J and K inputs and "decides" what
the next state should be. However, the Slave remains disabled (locked), so the final output Q does not change yet. Since Q
hasn't changed, the feedback sent back to the Master's inputs remains constant. This prevents the "looping" toggle that
causes the race condition. (ref slides/ref book)

Ans:
Propagation Delay is the time delay between the moment a signal transition occurs at the input (like the clock edge) and
the moment the resulting transition appears at the output. Every flip-flop has an inherent, non-zero propagation delay
dictated by the physical switching speed of its internal transistors. In a ripple counter, the state change has to propagate
through the entire chain of flip-flops sequentially. The accumulated delay directly dictates the speed limit of the ripple
counter. For the counter to operate reliably, the next clock pulse must not arrive until all flip-flops have had sufficient
time to settle to their new, correct state. Limiting Condition is the minimum time period allowed for the clock signal
